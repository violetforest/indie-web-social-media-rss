Follow websites, not accounts.

A mobile feed for the open web. You follow sites instead of profiles. When a site publishes, its RSS post lands in your feed, and you can comment on it, repost it and follow the people you meet there. The goal is to give people a reason to leave the big platforms without losing the news outlets, shows, bands and brands they follow there.

What all three share

  • Follow any websitePaste a URL, or the @handle an outlet uses on Instagram or X. The app finds their own site and its RSS feed.
  • No ranking algorithmPosts appear in the order they were published, and the feed ends.
  • Reading happens on the site“Read on site” opens the publisher's page, so they get the visit and the ad or subscription revenue.
  • Your site is your posting accountIf you have a blog, its posts show up as yours. No blog? A simple hosted one can come later.
  • Publishers can verifyA site owner proves ownership and replies with a badge.
  • Leave any timeImport and export your follows as OPML, the format every RSS reader understands.
  • Profiles you can decorateMySpace-style customization that stays on your profile and never touches anyone's feed.

Recommendation

Launch with Commons' feed and public comments, because familiarity is what gets people to switch. Leave out public like counts, as Tidepool does, so the feed doesn't drift back toward engagement chasing.

Add porches and webrings from Porchlight once people have friends on the app. Pools from Tidepool are cheap to add at any point, since they are only saved lists of posts.

Whichever model wins, MySpace-style profiles are worth building early. They are the most visible difference from the big platforms and the easiest thing to share.

Five names

Each one points the product in a slightly different direction.

  • Commonscommons.app?

    The open web as shared land instead of a platform's property. Plain and civic, and it sits well next to news outlets and brands.

    Used in Option A
  • Tidepooltidepool.social?

    New posts wash in with the tide, and each site is its own small ecosystem. A gentle nod to surfing the web.

    Used in Option B
  • Porchlightporchlight.app?

    A light on means someone is home. Warm and neighborly, and a good fit for small groups.

    Used in Option C
  • Webringwebring.social?

    Brings back the 90s loop of linked sites. People who remember them get it at once, and people who don't get curious.

  • Guestbookguestbook.app?

    Every post becomes a page you can sign. Puts comments and MySpace-era profiles at the center.

All five are common words, so other companies probably use some of them. Domains and app-store listings above are guesses and haven't been checked. Run a trademark and app-store search before committing to one.

Every site, person and post in these prototypes is invented. Profile customizations are saved only in your own browser.
